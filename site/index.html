THE IDEA

Learn broadly.
Adapt precisely.

Thin neuronal boundaries, weak membrane contrast, and changes in acquisition conditions make electron-microscopy instance segmentation difficult to reuse across datasets.

UniSEM links generalist learning and target specialization in a two-stage framework. In the first stage, NeuroStruct is trained on multi-source EM data. It combines the prompt-free dense prediction interface of µSAM with an HQ-inspired cross-layer correction branch to predict foreground, center distances, and boundary distances. In the second stage, the complete model is specialized on target data. LightFT freezes the original encoder weights while updating Q/V low-rank branches in all 24 encoder blocks together with the dense heads.

In the reported EMNeuron and WaferEM comparisons, UniSEM achieves lower overall VOI and ARAND than the listed external baselines. Structure and training-stage comparisons support the design. After source training, approximately one quarter of the target labels outperforms target-only training with all labels; LightFT reaches near-full-fine-tuning quality while updating approximately 4.1% as many parameters.

The evaluation concerns 2D instances within individual slices. Cross-slice identity reconstruction and general claims about all EM datasets are outside the reported evidence.

METHOD

A complete model, reused.

The first stage learns a reusable dense predictor.
The second stage learns a target expert.

01

NeuroStruct

A SAM-initialized ViT-L and dense decoder provide prompt-free predictions. An early/final-feature branch adds a three-channel logit correction.

02

LightFT

Retain the original encoder weights. Update Q/V LoRA in all 24 blocks, the dense decoder, and the correction branch to specialize the complete model.

03

From fields to instances

Overlapping predictions are blended; watershed fragments are merged with a route-specific random forest. The same output interface supports reuse.

Built on ideas from µSAM, HQ-SAM, SAM, and LoRA. UniSEM evaluates their task-specific integration, source-model reuse, and target specialization.

02 / MODEL DESIGN

Which dense structure works?

Combining early detail and final-layer context gives the lowest mean error among these four source-model configurations. A final-feature residual alone does not improve the dense decoder.

Why learn before specializing?

Source training provides a stronger starting point than official SAM with a random dense head. Target training then improves the transferred model on WaferEM. The two stages answer different needs.
